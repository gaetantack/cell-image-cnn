## Data
We used the Jurkat cell cycle imaging flow cytometry dataset (BBBC048) [CITE: Eulenberg 2017; CITE: Ljosa 2012 for BBBC]. It contains 32,266 single Jurkat cells imaged on an ImageStream cytometer, each as a 66 × 66 pixel, 8-bit image per channel, and labelled with one of seven phases: G1 (14,333 cells), S (8,616), G2 (8,601), prophase (606), metaphase (68), anaphase (15) and telophase (27). As model input we used only the two label-free channels, brightfield (Ch3) and darkfield/side scatter (Ch6), stacked as a 2-channel image. The fluorescence DNA channel (Ch4) was never used as input, because the phase labels were derived from it, so using it would leak the label into the input. Our main task has four classes: G1, S, G2 and mitosis, where the four mitotic phases are merged (716 cells). We merged them because anaphase and telophase have so few cells (15 and 27) that a held-out set would contain only about two and four of them. The seven-class task is reported as a secondary result.

## Data split
Cells were split once into training (70%, 22,586 cells), validation (15%, 4,840) and test (15%, 4,840) sets, stratified on the seven-class label, with a fixed seed (42); the indices are stored in the repository. The validation set contains 2,150 G1, 1,293 S, 1,290 G2 and 107 mitotic cells (91 prophase, 10 metaphase, 2 anaphase, 4 telophase). All model selection used the validation set; the test set was used once, for the final results only. **[All numbers in the current drafts are validation numbers.]**

## Hand-crafted features
For each cell, a mask was computed from the brightfield image. Because a cell in brightfield has both a dark rim and a bright interior on a grey background, we thresholded the absolute difference from the background level (median of a 3-pixel border ring) after Gaussian smoothing (σ = 1). The threshold was the lower of two Otsu thresholds [CITE: Otsu 1979], one computed on the whole image and one on the pixels within 25 pixels of the image centre. The mask was then closed (disc of radius 2), holes were filled, and objects of 30 pixels or fewer were removed. Objects that reached the border ring were dropped; if only such objects remained, the one nearest the centre was kept. From the mask we computed 28 features with scikit-image [CITE: van der Walt 2014]: seven shape features (area, perimeter, eccentricity, solidity, major and minor axis length, extent); the mean, standard deviation and 10th, 25th, 50th, 75th and 90th percentiles of brightfield intensity inside the mask; four grey-level co-occurrence matrix (GLCM) texture features (contrast, homogeneity, energy, correlation; 32 grey levels, distance 1, averaged over 0°, 45°, 90° and 135°) for each channel inside the mask; the darkfield standard deviation inside the mask; and the darkfield percentiles inside the mask minus the median darkfield level outside the mask. Raw darkfield intensity levels were excluded from the main feature set because the darkfield background level differs between phases and could act as a shortcut. One cell had an empty mask; its missing features were imputed with the training-set median of each feature.

## Models
*Parameter counts from `src.models.count_parameters`.*

- **Chance baselines.** A majority-class classifier and a stratified random classifier (scikit-learn `DummyClassifier`) fitted on the training labels.
- **Random forest.** A random forest of 500 trees [CITE: Breiman 2001] (scikit-learn [CITE: Pedregosa 2011]) on the 28 features, with median imputation fitted on the training rows only.
- **SmallCNN** (389,636 parameters for four classes). Four blocks of 3 × 3 convolution (32, 64, 128 and 256 filters, no bias), batch normalisation, ReLU and 2 × 2 max pooling, followed by global average pooling, dropout (p = 0.3) and a linear layer. Input 2 × 66 × 66.
- **ResNet18** [CITE: He 2016] (11,175,428 parameters for four classes), timm implementation [CITE: Wightman 2019], with the first convolution replaced by a 2-channel one. The 66 × 66 input is upscaled bilinearly to 128 × 128 inside the model. *Pretrained:* ImageNet weights (timm `resnet18.a1_in1k`) [CITE: Deng 2009]; each of the two input channels of the first convolution receives the mean of the three RGB filters, and the classification layer is newly initialised. *Scratch:* the same architecture with random initialisation.

## Training
CNNs were trained in PyTorch [CITE: Paszke 2019] with AdamW [CITE: Loshchilov 2019] and a cosine learning-rate schedule (updated every step, decaying to zero at the last epoch), with cross-entropy loss weighted by inverse class frequency in the training set (weight of class k = N / (K · n_k)). Each image channel was standardised with the mean and standard deviation of the training images. During training, each image was randomly rotated by a multiple of 90°, flipped horizontally and/or vertically, and its intensity jittered per channel (x · (1 + g) + b, with g and b uniform in ±0.1 in standardised units). After each epoch we computed the validation macro-F1, kept the weights of the best epoch, and stopped training if the validation macro-F1 had not improved for five epochs. All randomness (Python, NumPy, PyTorch, batch order and augmentation) was seeded. Training on the Apple MPS backend is not guaranteed to be bit-for-bit reproducible.

## Hyperparameter tuning
Each model family had the same budget of 12 configurations, evaluated on the validation set with seed 0, and the configuration with the highest validation macro-F1 was selected. Random forest: `max_features` ∈ {√p, 0.33·p} × `min_samples_leaf` ∈ {1, 5, 20} × `class_weight` ∈ {balanced, balanced_subsample}. CNNs: learning rate ∈ {1e-4, 5e-4, 2e-3} × weight decay ∈ {1e-4, 1e-2} × batch size ∈ {64, 128}, with augmentation and class weighting fixed. Because of the compute budget (one laptop), tuning runs were limited to 4 epochs and final runs to 12 epochs, identically for all CNN families. The selected configuration was then trained with seeds 0, 1 and 2. The seven-class models reused the configuration selected on four classes, without further tuning.

## Evaluation
We report macro-averaged F1, balanced accuracy (the mean of per-class recalls), per-class recall and row-normalised confusion matrices, computed with scikit-learn, as mean ± standard deviation over three seeds. Chance baselines are reported alongside each model. Training time is the wall-clock time of the training loop (including the per-epoch validation pass), measured on [HARDWARE: Apple M2, 8 GB, PyTorch MPS backend for CNNs; random forest on CPU, 8 cores].
